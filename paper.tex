\begin{abstract}
Machine learning on MRI and fluid biomarkers could support earlier detection
of Alzheimer's disease, but privacy regulation prevents hospitals from pooling
the data such models are trained on.  We present a federated, differentially
private and explainable pipeline for cognitively normal (CN), mild cognitive
impairment (MCI) and Alzheimer's disease (AD) classification, evaluated on
\CohSubjects{} subjects from \CohSites{} sites of the Alzheimer's Disease
Neuroimaging Initiative with subject-disjoint and site-disjoint
cross-validation.  Each subject is described by \CohFeatures{} features fixed in
advance from the AD literature: \CohRegions{} region volumes from neural
voxel-based morphometry, APOE4, amyloid PET, a tau and a neurodegeneration
composite of cerebrospinal fluid and plasma markers, and demographics, giving a
model with \CohDim{} parameters.  Trained with subject-level differential
privacy over the real acquisition sites, it reaches \DpBinOne{} on CN versus AD
(AUROC \DpBinOneAUROC{}) and \DpTriOne{} on the three-class task at a privacy
budget of one, within about one point of the best centralised non-private
model, while a membership-inference attack scores an AUROC of \AttackOne{}.
Under the same protocol a \CohMriDim{}-parameter model over all
\CohMriFeatures{} atlas regions reaches \MriBinOne{} and \MriTriOne{}, and its
region attributions agree with the non-private model's only weakly
(\XaiMriTriOne{} against \XaiTriOne{}): the cost of subject-level privacy, in
accuracy and in explanation, is set by the number of perturbed parameters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Structural MRI captures the atrophy that accompanies Alzheimer's disease, most
prominently in the medial temporal lobe~\cite{jack2010}, and machine learning
on MRI has been studied extensively as a route to earlier
diagnosis~\cite{cuingnet2011,wen2020}.  The biological definition of the
disease now rests on amyloid, tau and neurodegeneration
biomarkers~\cite{jack2018}, and blood tests such as plasma
p-tau217~\cite{palmqvist2020} are making them cheap to measure.  Models of
this kind need data from many hospitals, yet patient-privacy regulation makes
centralising them difficult.  Federated learning~\cite{mcmahan2017} trains a
shared model while the data stay at each site~\cite{kaissis2020,rieke2020},
but it does not by itself bound what the model reveals about any patient:
model updates and the released model can leak training
membership~\cite{shokri2017,yeom2018}.  Differential privacy
(DP)~\cite{dwork2014} provides that bound, at a cost in utility that is often
prohibitive for deep networks on small medical cohorts~\cite{ziller2021}.
Clinical use also requires that a model explain its decisions, and it is not
known how privacy affects those explanations.

This paper measures the three costs together, on the same subjects and under
the same protocol: what federating over real hospital sites costs, what
subject-level DP costs, and what privacy does to the model's explanations.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a compact representation of \CohFeatures{} named features, chosen a
  priori from the AD literature, that combines MRI region volumes with fluid
  and PET biomarkers measured in different subsets of subjects;
\item subject-level DP-FedAvg over the cohort's real acquisition sites that
  reaches \DpBinOne{} on CN versus AD and \DpTriOne{} on CN/MCI/AD at
  $\varepsilon = 1$, within about one point of the best non-private
  centralised model;
\item a measured instance of the dimension law of DP: the same protocol on all
  \CohMriFeatures{} atlas regions loses accuracy and, far more, explanation
  fidelity;
\item federated gradient boosting over the same sites, a membership-inference
  audit, and a test of generalisation to the newest ADNI scanners.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Classification from MRI and biomarkers.}  Benchmarks of structural-MRI
classifiers~\cite{cuingnet2011} and of convolutional networks~\cite{wen2020}
report that voxel-based features perform as well as deep networks for CN
versus AD, and that splitting data below the subject level overstates
accuracy~\cite{wen2020,bron2021}.  The blinded CADDementia challenge places
the best three-class accuracy from structural MRI at
63.0\%~\cite{bron2015}.  Amyloid PET is quantified on the common Centiloid
scale~\cite{klunk2015}, and plasma p-tau217 separates AD from other
conditions nearly as well as CSF or PET~\cite{palmqvist2020}.

\emph{Federated learning for Alzheimer's disease.}  Federated averaging and
secure aggregation have been applied to MRI-based Alzheimer's
detection~\cite{mitrovska2024}, to MCI-to-AD progression from multi-site
health records~\cite{pan2025}, and combined with rule-based reasoning under
uncertainty~\cite{basnin2025}.  FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} adds a proximal
term to limit client drift, and histogram-based gradient-boosted
trees~\cite{chen2016xgboost} can be federated over horizontally partitioned
data~\cite{li2023fedtree}.  These studies typically simulate client
heterogeneity; we federate over the sites that acquired the data.

\emph{Differential privacy in medical imaging.}  DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016}
bounds the influence of each training example, and user-level
DP-FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2018} bounds the influence of each participant.
Applied to deep networks on medical images, DP has been shown to cost
accuracy~\cite{ziller2021}.  We account for privacy with the privacy random
variable (PRV) accountant~\cite{gopi2021}, tighter than R\'enyi
DP~\cite{mironov2017}, as implemented in Opacus~\cite{opacus}, and audit it
with membership inference~\cite{shokri2017,yeom2018,carlini2022}.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Cohort}
\label{sec:cohort}

We use T1-weighted MRI from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative
(ADNI)~\cite{petersen2010}: \CohSubjects{} subjects (\CohCN{}~CN,
\CohMCI{}~MCI and \CohAD{}~AD) from \CohSites{} sites and every ADNI phase
(ADNI-1: \CohAdniOne{}, ADNI-GO: \CohAdniGo{}, ADNI-2: \CohAdniTwo{},
ADNI-3: \CohAdniThree{}, ADNI-4: \CohAdniFour{}).  Each subject contributes exactly one scan, so that the
subject is the unit of evaluation, and each diagnosis is the one recorded at
the visit of that scan.  No cognitive test score (MMSE, CDR, ADAS, FAQ, MoCA)
is ever an input, because ADNI assigns the diagnosis from them.

\subsection{Features}
\label{sec:features}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarises the pipeline.  Each scan's orientation is
recovered by registering all 48 axis permutations and reflections to an
ICBM152 template~\cite{fonov2011}; brains are extracted with
deepbet~\cite{fisch2023deepbet} and registered to MNI152 for quality
control~\cite{garyfallidis2014}.  Region volumes are produced by
deepmriprep~\cite{fisch2024deepmriprep}, a neural implementation of the CAT12
voxel-based morphometry pipeline~\cite{gaser2024}: grey matter, white matter
and CSF are segmented, and the Neuromorphometrics atlas is warped back so that
each of \CohMriFeatures{} regions is measured on the subject's own anatomy, as
a volume over total intracranial volume.

The model reads \CohFeatures{} features fixed before any experiment from the AD
literature~\cite{jack2010,jack2018}, not selected on this data.  The
\CohRegions{} region volumes are the left and right hippocampus, amygdala,
entorhinal area, parahippocampal, inferior and middle temporal and fusiform
gyri and precuneus (grey matter) and the lateral and inferior lateral
ventricles (CSF).  The others are APOE4 allele count, amyloid PET in
centiloids~\cite{klunk2015}, education, age and sex, and two composites.  The
\emph{tau composite} combines CSF p-tau181/A$\beta$42 (Roche Elecsys) with log
plasma p-tau217~\cite{palmqvist2020}; the \emph{neurodegeneration composite}
combines log plasma NfL and GFAP.  Each composite part is z-scored with the
training fold's mean and standard deviation and the parts a subject has are
averaged, so a subject with only a blood test and one with only a lumbar
puncture share one feature.  Coverage is incomplete (amyloid PET
\CovAmyloid{}, CSF \CovCsf{}, plasma p-tau217 \CovPtau{}, NfL and GFAP
\CovNfl{}, APOE4 \CovApoe{}); a missing value is set to the training-fold
median.

Centralised references are a soft-voting ensemble (logistic regression, RBF
support vector machine, shrinkage LDA and random forest) and a logistic
regression whose regularisation is tuned by nested cross-validation inside each
training fold.  Every configuration is evaluated out-of-fold with five-fold
cross-validation under two protocols: \emph{subject-disjoint}, stratified by
diagnosis, and \emph{site-disjoint}, which holds out whole acquisition sites
and so measures generalisation to an unseen scanner.  Each is repeated over
three seeds.  Standardisation, composites and imputation are fitted on the
training fold only.  We report accuracy, balanced accuracy and macro AUROC
(one-versus-rest), with the majority-class rate as chance.

\subsection{Federated Learning}
\label{sec:fl}

The federated model is a multinomial logistic regression over the
standardised features, with $(\CohFeatures{}+1)K$ parameters for $K$ classes
(\CohDim{} for three).  The natural partition assigns one client per ADNI
site and pools sites with fewer than eight subjects into one client, so that
no subject is discarded; a training fold yields \CohClients{} clients of
\CohClientMin{} to \CohClientMax{} subjects.  Training runs \CohRounds{}
rounds of \CohLocalEpochs{} local epochs with federated averaging
(FedAvg)~\cite{mcmahan2017} and with FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} ($\mu = 1$).
For comparison, federated gradient boosting grows histogram trees over all
\CohMriFeatures{} regions in three tissues and the biomarkers: each site
sends, per tree node, the summed gradient, hessian and count per feature bin,
and the server adds the site histograms and picks the
split~\cite{li2023fedtree}, so no subject row leaves a site.

We protect each subject rather than each scan or gradient
step~\cite{mcmahan2018}.  In every round each subject is sampled with
probability $q = \CohSampleRate{}$; each sampled subject's local update is
clipped to an $\ell_2$ norm of $C = \CohClip{}$; the clipped updates are summed
across all clients; and Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 C^2 I_d)$ is
added once to the sum before it is divided by the number of participants.
The PRV accountant~\cite{gopi2021,opacus} composes the privacy loss of the
sampled Gaussian mechanism over the \CohRounds{} rounds, and $\sigma$ is the
smallest noise multiplier for which it certifies $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-DP
with $\delta = \CohDelta{}$: from \CohSigmaOne{} at $\varepsilon = 1$ to
\CohSigmaTen{} at $\varepsilon = 10$, where the R\'enyi
bound~\cite{mironov2017} would need $\sigma = \CohSigmaOneRdp{}$ at
$\varepsilon = 1$.

The noise is isotropic in the $d$ perturbed parameters, so its expected norm
is $\mathbb{E}\,\lVert \boldsymbol{\xi} \rVert_2 \approx \sigma C \sqrt{d}$,
whereas the summed update of $N$ participating subjects is bounded by $NC$
irrespective of $d$.  The worst-case noise-to-signal ratio is therefore
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{ns}} \approx \frac{\sigma \sqrt{d}}{N},
\label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
which depends on the model only through $\sqrt{d}$.

\subsection{Explanation Fidelity and Membership Inference}
\label{sec:xai}

Because every feature is named, a linear model's attribution is a value per
feature, the summed magnitude of its weights over classes, so two models'
attributions are directly comparable.  We report the Spearman correlation
between the feature importance of each private model and that of the
non-private reference trained on the same folds, and the share of attribution
in the hippocampus and amygdala.  To audit privacy empirically we run a
subject-level loss-threshold membership-inference attack~\cite{yeom2018}:
training subjects are members, the held-out fold supplies non-members, and the
attack is scored by AUROC, for which 0.5 is chance.

Table~\ref{tab:dp} and Fig.~\ref{fig:privacy} report subject-level DP-FedAvg
over the real sites.  At $\varepsilon = 1$ the private model reaches
\DpBinOne{} on CN versus AD (AUROC \DpBinOneAUROC{}) and \DpTriOne{} on three
classes (balanced \DpTriOneBal{}, AUROC \DpTriOneAUROC{}), against
\DpBinNone{} and \DpTriNone{} without privacy and \CenBinBio{} and
\CenTriBio{} for the best centralised model.  At $\varepsilon = 10$ it reaches
\DpBinTen{} and \DpTriTen{}.  Strong privacy therefore costs about one point,
and the private models are no worse than the non-private one: at this size
the calibrated noise acts as a regulariser.

Table~\ref{tab:models} separates the sources of this result.  The paper's
earlier \CohMriDim{}-parameter region model scored \VsevTriOne{} and
\VsevBinOne{} at $\varepsilon = 1$ on 799 subjects.  On the present cohort the
same model reaches \MriTriOne{} and \MriBinOne{}, so more data and
visit-matched labels account for most of the gain; the compact model adds
the rest and, above all, keeps its explanations.  By~\eqref{eq:ratio} its
noise-to-signal ratio is \LawOursOne{} at $\varepsilon = 1$ and \LawOursTen{}
at $\varepsilon = 10$, against \LawHeadTen{} for a ResNet50 classification
head and \LawFullTen{} for a full ResNet50 at $\varepsilon = 10$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:law}).

\subsection{Explanation Fidelity Under Privacy}
\label{sec:res-xai}

Privacy changes what a model attends to far more than how accurate it is.
At $\varepsilon = 1$ the three-class attribution agreement of the
\CohFeatures{}-feature model with its non-private reference is \XaiTriOne{}
(\XaiTriTen{} at $\varepsilon = 10$); for the \CohMriFeatures{}-region model
on the same subjects it is \XaiMriTriOne{}, and for the earlier model
\XaiVsevTriOne{}, essentially unrelated to its non-private reference although
its accuracy barely moved.  An accuracy-only privacy--utility curve cannot
reveal this; a small, named representation is what keeps a private model
explicable.

\subsection{Membership Inference and Scanner Shift}
\label{sec:res-mia}

Against the non-private federated three-class model the subject-level attack
reaches an AUROC of \AttackNone{}; at $\varepsilon = 10$ it is \AttackTen{}
and at $\varepsilon = 1$ \AttackOne{}, at chance.  To test the newest
scanners, the centralised model was trained on ADNI-1 to ADNI-3 only and tested
on the \ShiftN{} ADNI-4 subjects: it reaches \ShiftTri{} on three classes
(\ShiftBin{} on CN versus AD) against \MixedTri{} (\MixedBin{}) when ADNI-4
subjects from other folds are in training, so the newer protocols cost
nothing.

% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three findings are likely to hold beyond this cohort.  First, the cost of
subject-level differential privacy is a property of the perturbed dimension:
the noise norm grows as $\sqrt{d}$ while the summed update does not, so a
compact representation is what makes a strong privacy budget affordable.
Second, that compactness can come from domain knowledge rather than from the
data: \CohFeatures{} features named in advance lose almost nothing to privacy.
Third, explanation fidelity should be reported beside accuracy whenever a model
is trained privately, because a large private model can be as accurate and
far less explicable.

\emph{Limitations.}  The three-class task remains far from clinical
reliability, because MCI is heterogeneous.  Biomarker coverage is partial and
imputed, and although the features were named in advance, the form of the two
composites was settled after comparing a few variants under the same
cross-validation, so the reported accuracy may be slightly optimistic.
Education enters ADNI's memory-test cut-offs and may carry some label
information.  The federation runs in one process; feature standardisation and
imputation statistics are cohort aggregates outside the privacy guarantee.
All data come from ADNI, so other populations require external validation.
Nothing here is a medical device or a diagnosis.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a federated, differentially private and explainable pipeline for
Alzheimer's disease classification from MRI and fluid biomarkers.  A model of
\CohDim{} parameters over \CohFeatures{} literature-chosen features, trained
with subject-level DP-FedAvg over \CohSites{} real ADNI sites, keeps
\DpBinOne{} on CN versus AD and \DpTriOne{} on CN/MCI/AD at $\varepsilon = 1$,
about one point below the best non-private model, while a membership-inference
attack is at chance (AUROC \AttackOne{}) and its explanations stay close to the
non-private model's.  The privacy cost is governed by the number of perturbed
parameters.  The code, folds and aggregate results are publicly available, and
a public demonstration serves only the $\varepsilon = 1$ private model.
